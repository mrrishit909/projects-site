# Resume bullets: InvoiceBridge (P05-ART-10)

Based on what was built and verified (the verification ledger, `uat-evidence.md`, this package's reconciliation and tests) and consistent with the public case study (`/invoicebridge/project.json`). The project uses **synthetic data only**; no bullet claims business impact, adoption, time or cost savings, or users.
Professional facts outside this project belong to the resume, not to this file. Wording stays inside the case study: no figure appears here that is not in it or in `sql-analysis.md`.

* Specified an accounts-payable matching and approval contract in nine written decisions (D-01..D-09): invoice identity as vendor, normalised number, date and computed total with identical source bytes caught first; decimal money with tax computed once per invoice; a cumulative three-way match against net receipts; approval by a different person under the policy version in force; idempotent export batches; credit notes applied once. A hand-derived known-answer fixture (an order for 10 at 20.00, a receipt of 6, an invoice for 6 giving 120.00 before tax and 132.00 with tax, a blocked second invoice for 4, a re-sent invoice merged) is implemented independently in Python, TypeScript and PostgreSQL SQL.
* Designed a PostgreSQL 16 schema in three plain-SQL migrations (immutable extracted source, append-only import lineage, assignments, approvals and export rows) and FastAPI commands with actor-and-role-bound idempotency, row locks, 401 / 403 / 409 / 422 contracts, a stale-dataset-version check and 501 for the unbuilt extraction correction (C06); verified by a 402-test local suite and, on earlier commits, by GitHub-hosted CI runs.
* Triaged and fixed the 22 findings of an independent review (R5-01..R5-22): committed invoices now consume received quantity first and are never displaced, money is reported per currency instead of summed, decisions are stamped by the server, and a reversal after export surfaces as an exception; every fix has regression tests in Python, SQL, TypeScript and the API, and the reviewer's fifteen hand-calculated cases agree across all three implementations.
* Wrote eight executable SQL investigations on a seeded synthetic snapshot (match outcomes by vendor and reason with "open at the cutoff" kept apart from "ever raised", cumulative consumption per purchase-order line at three cutoffs, duplicates by identity against bytes with month-end clustering, approvals under two policy versions with separation of duties, exception backlog and assignment history, export batches and reconciliation with exclusions, credits and payable per currency, rejections and injected defects), each figure reconciled through 286 reconciliation rows to an independent SQL reference and the Python domain.
* Built a formula-driven analyst workbook (live formulas per currency and a three-cutoff switch) recalculated by LibreOffice headless and compared with the domain, and a BI package (star schema, CSV exports, DAX-style measures re-implemented in Python and reconciled); stated plainly that Excel and Power BI Desktop were not run and that no `.pbix` exists.
* Wrote an executive memo that keeps observed, simulated and hypothetical results apart and discloses the generator's choices (it approves only uninterrupted matches, rejects only never-matched invoices and closes each period with a batch, so the zero export reconciliation is by construction): per-currency evidence, the vendor group that invoices before receipt (163 of 538 invoices blocked at the cutoff, 524 at some point) and the vendor whose price list changed (30 of the 32 open price variances), with the model's stated limits; packaged with a data dictionary, UAT evidence, a technical case study and a checksummed release package with a rollback manifest.
