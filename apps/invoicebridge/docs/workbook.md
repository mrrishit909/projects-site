# Analyst workbook (P05-ART-05)

File: `artifacts/invoicebridge-analyst-workbook.xlsx` (built by `artifacts/build_workbook.py` with openpyxl 3.1.5, pinned in `requirements-artifacts.txt`). All data is **synthetic**: the published snapshot `ib-20261026-20261026`
(seed 20261026, generator `dataset_hash` `1f001a43a7031d4260f5abb3b9f7a83f6e1b48397e85cf8b04e0b4d9e052162e`, contract v1, code revision `2dcc460`). Everything in the workbook is OBSERVED in that synthetic history; it contains no simulation and no hypothetical benefit, and the history it describes was generated (the generator approves only uninterrupted matches, rejects only never-matched invoices and closes each period with a batch of everything approved, so the approval mix, the rejection mix and the zero export difference are not findings).

The workbook lets an analyst follow, cell by cell, how 2,800 imported invoices, 5,322 lines, 2,356 approval decisions, 5,067 receipts and 59 export requests become the headline metrics of the contract, **per currency** (USD, EUR, GBP), and how they move between three cutoffs: the March month-end, the June month-end and the as-of 2026-10-01T02:00:00Z. One switch cell, `Cutoff!B3`, takes `march`, `june` or `asof`.
It also reproduces the known answer of the golden fixture (a purchase order for 10 at 20.00, a receipt of 6, an invoice of 6 giving 120.00 + 12.00 = 132.00, a blocked second invoice of 4, a duplicate merged) with formulas.
**Money is never summed across currencies:** `Metrics` has one column per currency and the all-currency column carries counts and units only.

**How to read it without installing anything:** open the `.xlsx` in any spreadsheet application (the delivered file already carries values), or read the table below and the expected results.

## Recalculation status (stated plainly)
* **openpyxl cannot calculate.** The builder writes formulas only (134,948 of them), so the file it writes first has no cached values.
* **LibreOffice 24.2.7.2 is installed here** (`/usr/bin/soffice`). The build ran `soffice --headless --convert-to xlsx` and the delivered file is that recalculated output: it keeps its formulas and carries LibreOffice-computed cached values. Read with openpyxl `data_only=True`, `Metrics` equals the values above and `Metrics!E3` is `TRUE` for all three positions of the switch.
* **Excel itself was NOT run.** Nothing here was recalculated or opened by Microsoft Excel. The formulas use only functions both engines share (`SUMIFS`, `COUNTIFS`, `SUM`, `INDEX`, `MATCH`, `IFERROR`, `IF`, `AND`, `OR`, `ROUND`, `MAX`, `ABS`, `ISNUMBER`, `SEARCH`, `CHOOSE`). LibreOffice drops the `fullCalcOnLoad` flag, so the builder writes `fullCalcOnLoad="1"` back into `xl/workbook.xml` after the conversion (a test reads it); that Excel honours it was not observed.
* `tests/test_artifacts.py` proves the same independently of any spreadsheet engine and of the delivered cache: it checks that every result cell of the formula sheets is a formula (the typed exceptions are the facts, the policies, the tolerance, the typed match outcomes and the expected values of the check columns), reads the cached values and compares them with the **domain recomputed in the test** (not with the pasted column), compares the total of **every one of the 2,800 invoices** with the domain, recalculates a **copy** with LibreOffice as delivered, and recalculates two more copies with `Cutoff!B3` set to `june` and `march` (formulas only, so no cache) to prove the switch is live and equals the domain's figures for those cutoffs.

## Limits
* **The three-way match chain is not replayed in the spreadsheet (W-02).** Whether an invoice matched at a cutoff, and with which reasons, depends on the cumulative consumption of the invoices before it in a particular order (committed invoices first, then invoice date, import instant and id) and is **typed per invoice and cutoff from the domain**; a wrong outcome would not be caught by this file alone. Everything after that outcome is a formula: amounts, validity of each approval, the first valid approval, the export rows, credits, the consumption of each PO line, the backlog and the rates. `docs/sql-analysis.md` recomputes the same outcomes from facts in SQL and reconciles them.
* **Floating point (W-03).** Line amounts are double products; subtotal and tax add 1E-11 before `ROUND` so exact half-cent ties round up as the domain's ROUND_HALF_UP does. All 2,800 totals equal the domain after recalculation (checked), but a tie within 1E-11 below a half cent would round up wrongly; none exists in this data.
* **The stored exception history is the nightly run's (W-05).** The derived open exceptions equal the stored history at the June month-end and the as-of and differ by one at the March month-end; `Metrics` shows both (`open_exceptions` and `open_exceptions_history`).
* **A policy limit is compared with the invoice total in its own currency (W-06)**, with no conversion; a reader must not read a USD and a GBP total as comparable.
* **Per-currency duplicates and the all-currency money are not shown:** a merge is counted over all currencies, and money is never added across them.
* **Recalculation runs in about 20 seconds in LibreOffice**; the workbook is 2 MB.
* `Domain reference` and `SQL results (pasted)` are pastes and go stale if the data or the queries change; their provenance is in the headers.
