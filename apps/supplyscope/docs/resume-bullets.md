# Resume bullets: SupplyScope (P03-ART-10)

Based on what was built and verified (verification ledger, this package's tests and runs). The project uses **synthetic data only**; no bullet claims business impact, adoption, time or cost savings, or users.
Professional facts outside this project belong to the resume, not to this file.

* Specified an inventory-position and replenishment contract (day order of receipts, demand and review; position = on hand + confirmed on order - backorders; MOQ applied before pack rounding; six written decisions D-01..D-06) with a hand-derived golden fixture (position 60 gives an order of 72, position 80 gives none), and implemented it independently in Python, TypeScript and two PostgreSQL idioms plus a spreadsheet; the sources agree on the fixture in both states.
* Designed a 28-table PostgreSQL 16 schema in two plain-SQL migrations with trigger-enforced immutable stock movements, receipt rules (no receipt before its order, none above the ordered quantity) and a backorder-or-lost-sale exclusion, and FastAPI commands with idempotent replay, 409 and 422 business errors, 501 for the unbuilt command and an audit row in the same transaction as each command; verified by a 132-test local suite and GitHub-hosted CI runs.
* Wrote eight executable SQL investigations on a seeded snapshot (month-end stock and position by location, the draft buying plan with its arithmetic, fill rate and stockout days by location and ABC class, supplier on-time rate by month computed only from receipts up to each cutoff, censored against latent demand, working capital at the cost in force across a cost step, forecast WAPE for baselines against a model, rejections by reason), each reconciled to an independent reference through 91 checks.
* Distinguished two stockout-day metrics that a reviewer found conflated (distinct calendar dates over calendar days, 290 of 548, against SKU x location x day cells, 431 of 32,880) and published both with their definitions in the SQL, the Power BI design and the memo.
* Built a formula-driven analyst workbook (running stock with the day-order sort key and an as-of filter, position, MOQ and pack rounding as live formulas, a position-80 switch) and validated it by LibreOffice headless recalculation, including copies with the switch flipped and the as-of moved, plus a separate Python evaluation of the same chain; stated plainly that Excel was not run.
* Packaged the work for review: data dictionary and ER diagram, a Power BI star schema with twelve DAX measures (no `.pbix` produced), an executive memo that states its options, uncertainty and what the synthetic data cannot show, a technical case study that lists the defects and reviewer findings with their status, and checksummed release artifacts.
