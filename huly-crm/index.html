01 / The problem

A small studio coordinating customer projects wants boards, a place to meet, and shared documents that several people edit at once, without conflicts or lost work.

Defining workflow: Two people join a room and see each other; a task move and chat messages propagate live; both edit one document concurrently and converge; the realtime process restarts and everything reconnects with nothing lost or duplicated.

02 / What I built

One of eleven independent CRM MVPs in a single monorepo, each with its own database, migrations, tests and review packet. This one is inspired by Huly and is called Hearth.

  • Kanban projects with drag and drop, assignees, due dates, comments, customer linkage and versioned edits
  • Virtual office rooms with authenticated presence (heartbeat, 30 second stale expiry) and persisted chat
  • A real authenticated WebSocket process: single-use signed tickets, origin check, per-message authorization, bounded frames
  • Collaborative documents on a maintained CRDT library (Yjs), persisted before broadcast, with snapshots and reconnect resync

03 / How it was built

  1. 01

    Specify before building

    An acceptance document names the persona, the bounded scope, what is deferred, and ties every required feature to evidence (a test, an end-to-end journey or a recorded check).

  2. 02

    Model the database so the rules hold even if the code is wrong

    A Prisma schema plus a hand-written migration of CHECK constraints, composite foreign keys that pin every record to its workspace, and triggers. The tests try to break these from raw SQL.

  3. 03

    Build the distinguishing workflow through every layer

    Kanban projects with drag and drop, assignees, due dates, comments, customer linkage and versioned edits

  4. 04

    Prove it in a fresh clone

    20 gate checks pass and 0 fail in a fresh clone of commit 2a04b27058: 2 unit, 32 integration against a real PostgreSQL, 19 browser tests on the production build, container start, a live black-box check, restart persistence, backup and restore, and a production-dependency audit.

  5. 05

    Review it as an independent reader would

    A review packet lists what is implemented, what is measured, what was fixed on the way, and every known limit and release blocker, so nothing here is claimed beyond its evidence.

05 / What I'd do next

  • No video, voice, screen share or call controls (explicit post-MVP boundary)
  • Plain-text collaborative documents (no rich text or remote cursors)
  • One realtime instance: presence and chat fan-out are in process memory; not serverless-compatible
  • Not production-ready: no TLS (wss), proxy or monitoring provisioned
