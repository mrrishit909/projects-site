How Fairly Do Votes Become Seats in India?

In 16 Lok Sabha elections (1962-2024) the party with the most seats beat its vote share by 14 points on average (28 in 1984, 7 in 2024); the Gallagher index ran from 5.4 to 22.0, and 52% of winners had under half the valid votes.

Python · pandas · Gallagher index · Effective number of parties · Margin-of-victory analysis · TCPD Lok Dhaba (non-commercial, cite) · ECI 2024 report (academic use) · Hand-written recomputeCode on GitHub ↗

Unopposed seats (13) are left out of every index and Surat 2024 is not in the ECI report. Parties are labels as recorded, not alliances (the 1977 Janata alliance is under BLD). Boundaries changed in 1967, 1977 and 2009, so there is no seat-level comparison over time; 1951-57 (two-member seats) is left out. Sources: TCPD Lok Dhaba, 1962-2019 (free for non-commercial use with citation; aggregates only); ECI 2024 constituency report via a third-party GitHub copy (academic use per its disclaimer; ECI's site answered scripted requests with HTTP 406, so nothing was bypassed).

01 — The problem

In a first-past-the-post election the candidate with the most votes wins, so the seats a party gets are not its share of the votes. How fairly do votes become seats in India's Lok Sabha elections, and how many winners have the support of less than half of their voters?

The task follows GeeksforGeeks' "Indian Election Data Analysis". The data is the constituency-level result of every general election from 1962 to 2024: 8,537 contested seats and 96,112 candidates, public election records, with no individual candidate named or profiled.

02 — What I built

Sixteen elections, counted seat by seat:

  • The audit: by-elections, delayed polls, unopposed seats, NOTA, and the columns of the two sources that disagree in definition.
  • The arithmetic: the seat bonus of the largest party, the Gallagher index, and the effective number of parties for votes and for seats.
  • The seats: winning margins, close races, winners under 50%, and vote splitting.
  • Who stands: independents, NOTA, turnout, women candidates and winners.
  • Check: check.py recounts all 3,340 numbers in plain Python, with no data or statistics library.

03 — How it was built

  1. 01

    Download two public files

    The Trivedi Centre for Political Data's Lok Sabha file for 1962 to 2019 (free for non-commercial use with the TCPD citation; the repository has no licence file, so only aggregates are published), and the Election Commission's 2024 constituency report. ECI's own site answers scripted requests with HTTP 406, so the 2024 file comes from a third-party GitHub copy and nothing was bypassed (4.4 MB, fetched and never committed, with checksums).

    "All_States_GE.csv.gz": "https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tcpd/tcpd-ld-data-archive/main/downloads/All_States/All_States_GE.csv.gz",
  2. 02

    Audit before counting

    2,818 by-election rows are dropped (68 of them show a turnout above 100%, up to 15,301%), the delayed 1985 and 1992 polls are folded into their Houses, 13 unopposed seats are set aside, and NOTA is kept out of party and candidate counts. The ECI file's "Valid Votes" excludes NOTA while TCPD's includes it, so every total is rebuilt from candidate rows; its party seat totals match ECI's (BJP 239 of 542, INC 99).

    quality["tcpd_bypoll_rows_turnout_over_100"] = int((by.Turnout_Percentage > 100).sum())
  3. 03

    Compute the seat arithmetic

    For each election: every party's share of the votes and of the seats, the Gallagher index of disproportionality (the square root of half the sum of squared gaps, in points), the effective number of parties for votes and for seats, and the seat bonus of the party with the most seats. Independents are one unit; splitting them lowers the index by at most 1.2 points.

    gall = math.sqrt(0.5 * ((pt.vote_share - pt.seat_share) ** 2).sum())
  4. 04

    Count seat by seat

    In each contested seat: the winner's margin over the best non-NOTA runner-up as a share of the seat's valid votes, whether the winner had under 50%, and whether the winner had fewer votes than the second and third together (arithmetic only, since votes do not transfer one for one between candidates).

    "split_n": int((W.w < W.r + W.third).sum())
  5. 05

    Recount it all without the libraries

    check.py reads the files with the csv and gzip modules and recomputes every index, count and audit figure with plain loops: 3,340 numbers to a relative tolerance of 1e-9, counts exact.

    gall = math.sqrt(0.5 * sum((vsh[p] - ssh[p]) ** 2 for p in pv))

04 — Results

The largest party gets a bonus. In 14 of 16 elections the party with the most seats had a seat share more than half a point above its vote share, by 13.8 points on average. The table gives the largest party's votes and seats (share of contested seats) and the Gallagher index, the square root of half the sum of squared gaps between vote and seat shares over all parties. In 1989 Congress had 39.5% of the votes and 37.3% of the seats; in 2004 the bonus was 0.2 points.

Seats are far from votes, and the distance moves. The Gallagher index runs from 5.4 (2004) to 22.0 (1984). In 1996, 1998 and 1999 Congress had the most votes (28.8%, 25.8%, 28.3%) but the BJP the most seats, counting party labels, not alliances. The effective number of parties (one over the sum of squared shares) is 3.4 to 7.6 for votes and 1.7 to 6.5 for seats; in 2014 the votes were split among 6.8 effective parties and the seats among 3.5. Dotted lines mark a change of constituency boundaries.

Half of the winners have under half of the votes, and a quarter of races are close. Across the 8,537 contested seats, 52.0% were won with less than 50% of the valid votes (52.8% in 2024, 286 seats, or 263 with NOTA left out of the denominator), 22.8% by under 5 points and 4.5% by under 1 point (388 seats). In the table, Median is the median winning margin in points of the seat's valid votes (the winner's lead over the best runner-up), Close the share of seats won by under 5 points, and Under 50% the share of winners with less than half of the valid votes.

Splitting, independents and the 1996 ballot. In 9% to 60% of seats the winner had fewer votes than the second and third combined (33.6% in 2024); that is arithmetic, not a claim about where those votes would have gone. Independents are 24% to 76% of the candidates but at most 13.8% of the votes, and 98.4% or more of them lost their deposit from 1980 to 2019. In 1996 a seat had 25.7 candidates on average, and Nalgonda had 480; in 1998 the average was 8.7.

Turnout, NOTA and women. Valid votes were 53.5% of electors in 1971, 58.2% in 2009 and 67.3% in 2019 (66.0% in 2024). NOTA took 1.1%, 1.1% and 1.0% of the valid votes in 2014, 2019 and 2024. Women were 3.7% of candidates in 1962 and 9.6% in 2024 and 7.3% and 13.7% of winners (78 women in 2019, 74 in 2024). A woman candidate was more likely to win than a man in all 16 elections (9.2% against 6.2% in 2024), a comparison not adjusted for party or for independents.
